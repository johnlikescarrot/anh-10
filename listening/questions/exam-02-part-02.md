## Câu hỏi và đáp án

**Question 6.** According to Dr. Minh's survey, how many evenings a week do busy city families eat dinner together on average?

- A. One evening
- B. Four evenings
- C. Six evenings
- D. Every evening

**Đáp án: B** — four evenings a week on average

**Question 7.** What does Dr. Minh identify as the biggest distraction during modern family meals?

- A. Loud street traffic
- B. Cooking complicated dishes
- C. Smartphones and television screens
- D. Unexpected phone calls from teachers

**Đáp án: C** — smartphones and television screens

**Question 8.** How can teenagers contribute before dinner starts?

- A. By setting the table and washing vegetables
- B. By ordering fast food every night
- C. By repairing kitchen appliances
- D. By doing all the heavy shopping alone

**Đáp án: A** — washing vegetables and setting the table

**Question 9.** According to Dr. Minh, teenagers who regularly eat with their parents tend to have ______.

- A. more Pocket money
- B. better communication skills and lower stress
- C. fewer household duties
- D. longer summer holidays

**Đáp án: B** — stronger communication skills and much lower stress

**Question 10.** What simple rule does Dr. Minh recommend to every family?

- A. Eat dinner in under ten minutes
- B. Keep meals "screen-free" for thirty minutes
- C. Cook only Western dishes on Sundays
- D. Let parents do all the washing-up

**Đáp án: B** — keep the dinner table screen-free for thirty minutes

## Tapescript

```text
Host: Welcome to Family Matters! Today Dr. Hoang Minh is sharing findings from his recent survey on urban family meals. Dr. Minh, how often do city families eat dinner together nowadays?
Dr. Minh: Thank you! Because of evening classes and overtime work, busy city families in our survey share dinner only about four evenings a week on average.
Host: And when they do sit down together, what gets in the way of conversation?
Dr. Minh: Definitely smartphones and television screens. When everyone stares at notifications or video clips, family members sit at the same table yet barely talk to one another.
Host: How can tenth-grade teenagers help make family meals warmer?
Dr. Minh: Teenagers don't need to cook complicated feasts. Simply washing vegetables and setting the table before dinner shows care and gratitude. Our study also found that teenagers who chat regularly with their parents over dinner develop stronger communication skills and experience much lower school stress.
Host: What is one golden rule every household can start tonight?
Dr. Minh: Keep the dinner table completely "screen-free" for just thirty minutes. That half hour builds lifelong family bonds!
```
