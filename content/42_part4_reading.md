## 4.5. Reading 1 – Cloze (đọc tờ rơi, điền từ)

### Bài đọc 1 – Tờ rơi câu lạc bộ

>. **GREEN DAY – JOIN US!**
> Do you care about the (1) ____ ? Our Go Green Club is organising a clean-up day on Sunday 12 October. We will (2) ____ plastic bags and rubbish along the river. Everyone is welcome, (3) ____ you need to register before Friday. Please bring a pair of gloves and a reusable water bottle. You do not need to bring food (4) ____ the club will provide a free lunch. Last year, more than 200 volunteers took part and we (5) ____ over one tonne of rubbish. Come and help us make our city greener!

**Chọn phương án đúng nhất cho mỗi chỗ trống (1–5).**

1.
A. environment
B. environmental
C. environmentally
D. environmentalist

2.
A. collect
B. collecting
C. collection
D. collective

3.
A. and
B. so
C. but
D. because

4.
A. although
B. because
C. so
D. but

5.
A. collect
B. collected
C. were collecting
D. have collect

**Đáp án:** [[KEY 1A 2A 3C 4B 5B]]

>+ **Vì sao**: 1. sau *the* → **danh từ** → *environment*. 2. sau *will* → **động từ nguyên mẫu** → *collect*. 3. *Everyone is welcome* ↔ *you need to register* là hai ý **tương phản** → *but*. 4. *You do not need to bring food* + lý do → *because*. 5. *Last year* → **quá khứ đơn** → *collected*.

## 4.6. Reading 2 – Đọc hiểu đoạn văn

### Bài đọc 1 – Family life

>. In the past, most Vietnamese families followed a very traditional pattern: the father was the breadwinner and the mother stayed at home to take care of the children and the house. Today, this picture has changed considerably. More women work outside the home, and many of them earn as much as, or even more than, their husbands. As a result, the idea that housework is only a woman's job no longer makes sense.
>.
> According to a recent survey of 500 families in big cities, nearly 70 per cent of young couples now share household chores. In these families, cooking, cleaning and shopping are divided according to each person's free time rather than their gender. Men are also more willing to take part in looking after children, which helps build a stronger bond between fathers and their sons and daughters.
>.
> However, the change is not complete. In many rural areas, women still spend about twice as much time on housework as men. Some people argue that traditional attitudes are hard to change because they have existed for generations. Others believe that schools and the media can play an important role in teaching young people that sharing housework is a sign of responsibility, not of weakness.
>.
> Whatever the reasons, one thing is clear: when all members contribute to the home, everyone benefits – the house is cleaner, the parents are less stressed, and children learn valuable skills for their future.

Thu nhập **không** được nêu là lợi ích → D. 5. *"In many rural areas, women still spend about twice as much time on housework as men"* → B.

### Bài đọc 2 – Music

>. Music is often called the universal language, and for good reason. Wherever we go in the world, we can enjoy a melody even if we do not understand a single word of the lyrics. Scientists have found that listening to music activates many different areas of the brain at the same time, which explains why a familiar song can bring back a memory so clearly.
>.
> For teenagers, music plays an especially important role. A survey of 1,000 students aged 15 to 18 showed that they listen to music for about two and a half hours a day. Most of them said they turned to music when they felt stressed, bored or lonely. Some also use English songs to improve their vocabulary and pronunciation, because repeating the lyrics helps them remember new words in a natural way.
>.
> However, not all music has the same effect. Songs with aggressive lyrics or a very fast beat can make listeners feel more anxious, while calm melodies can slow the heart rate and help people relax. This is why some hospitals now play soft music before operations.
>.
> Vietnamese folk music, unfortunately, is becoming less popular among young people. Only one in ten students in the survey said they listened to folk songs regularly. Yet folk music contains the history and identity of a nation, and many teachers believe that schools should do more to bring it closer to the young. Learning to appreciate our own music does not mean refusing modern songs; it simply means knowing where we come from.
